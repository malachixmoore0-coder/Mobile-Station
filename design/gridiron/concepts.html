01

Saturday Night Broadcast

The graphics package, not the game. Everything looks like it was keyed over a live feed thirty seconds before kickoff — hard diagonal cuts, condensed italics, a gold rail under the headline number.

  • Primetime
  • Kickoff
  • keep: Gridiron AI

Typography

GEORGIA 27.4 — ALABAMA 24.1

Saira Condensed at 800, skewed seven degrees, for every number that matters. Barlow carries the reading text. Condensed display against a normal-width body is the oldest trick in sports television and it still works.

Saira Condensed 800 · Barlow 400/600

Palette

Field#070B14
Panel#101A2E
Gold#FFC233
Home#38E08A
Away#FF6B3D

Midnight navy under a single stadium gold. Green and orange are not decoration — they are the home and away sides, and they appear nowhere else, so a glance at any chart tells you whose number it is.

UI & layout

  • NavFive bottom tabs stay. A scorebug pins to the top of every screen once a matchup is loaded.
  • ResultOpens full-bleed like a broadcast wipe. Win probability fills the first screen with no card around it.
  • SectionsAngled dividers instead of rounded cards. Three-pixel radius, everywhere, no exceptions.
  • MotionNumbers count up on load. The probability bar sweeps in from the favourite’s side.

Signature move

The persistent scorebug. Once you run a matchup it never leaves the top of the screen — browse a roster, open a player, tune the model, and the projected score and win probability update live above you. The app becomes a broadcast you are directing.

02

The Quant Desk

Stop pretending it is a sports app. It is a simulation terminal that happens to be pointed at football. Monospace top to bottom, amber phosphor on black, density over comfort, and a genuine respect for the reader’s intelligence.

  • Snapcount
  • The Desk
  • Vig

Typography

P(UGA) = 0.614 ± 0.008

One family, IBM Plex Mono, at four weights. Everything aligns because everything is the same width. No display face at all — hierarchy comes from weight, case and the amber, never from size jumps.

IBM Plex Mono 400/500/600/700

Palette

Void#0A0A0A
Row#131313
Phosphor#E8E4D9
Amber#FFB000
Long#3DD68C
Short#FF4D4D

Black terminal, warm phosphor white, amber for anything the model produced itself. Green and red mean direction only — above the line, below the line. Six colours and not a seventh.

UI & layout

  • NavTab bar deleted. A command bar sits at the bottom: type UGA BAMA and it runs.
  • ResultOne continuous readout. No cards, no sections — rules and indentation carry the structure.
  • DensityRoughly twice the information per screen. Twelve-pixel type, tight leading, zero radius.
  • DetailEvery figure shows its uncertainty. The seed is printed. Re-roll is a keystroke.

Signature move

The command bar. Two team codes and a return key runs ten thousand games. Add flags inline — UGA BAMA -neutral -wind — and the context chips other apps make you tap through become something you type once. It is the fastest possible path from thought to number.

Coach’s Chalkboard

Slate green board, a stick of chalk, and a room with no windows. The warmest of the ten and the only one that feels handmade — the model’s output arrives as if someone drew it for you at seven in the morning.

  • Chalk
  • Install
  • Whiteboard

Typography

Georgia by three — take the under

Caveat, a real chalk hand, for headlines and annotations only. Barlow Condensed does all the work underneath, because a whole app in a handwriting face is a joke that stops being funny on screen two.

Caveat 700 · Barlow Condensed 500/600

Palette

Slate#1C2621
Dust#3A4A41
Chalk#F2EFE4
Yellow#F2D06B
Home#7FD4A8

Board green, chalk white, and one stick of yellow kept for the line that matters. Nothing is fully saturated and nothing is pure white — every colour reads dusted on rather than printed.

UI & layout

  • NavTabs become tabs on a folder — the row sits at the top, magnet-style, not the bottom.
  • MatchupThe builder is a board. Drag the two team magnets on, toggle conditions, hit Install.
  • ResultSections are index cards taped to the board, slightly rotated, in reading order.
  • NarrativeThe three-act game script is written in chalk with arrows drawn between the acts.

Signature move

The advantage matrix drawn as a play. Instead of four bar rows, the four categories sit at their real positions on a diagrammed formation — trenches on the line, passing over the top — and the winning side’s circles are filled. You read the mismatch the way a coordinator would.

Field Level

Nine o’clock at night, standing on the grass, looking up into the lights. Almost everything is in shadow; the only bright thing on screen is the number the model produced. Quiet, spacious, and the most premium of the ten.

  • Hashmark
  • Field Level
  • Under the Lights

Typography

61.4% — Georgia

Outfit alone, from 300 to 800, tracked tight at display sizes. A single geometric family with a wide weight range gives you hierarchy without ever changing voice — the restraint is the point.

Outfit 300/400/600/800

Palette

Night#0B1410
Glass#132018
Halogen#FFE9AE
Turf#39C97E
Away#E8B44C

Grass so dark it reads black, with one warm halogen white that behaves like a light source rather than a colour. Everything else sits several stops down, the way things do under stadium lighting.

UI & layout

  • NavA floating pill dock, not a bar. It dims to twenty percent while you scroll.
  • ResultVertical panels you swipe, one idea per screen, like a story. Depth over length.
  • SurfacesFrosted glass over a blurred field photograph. Large radii, generous padding.
  • TypeThe win probability gets an entire screen to itself before anything else appears.

Signature move

The swipeable result story. Instead of one long scroll, the analysis becomes seven full-screen panels — probability, matrix, nodes, script, sleepers, injuries, distribution — each with room to breathe and a progress rail down the side. It turns a report into something you move through.

05

Gameday Program

The four-dollar program you buy walking into the stadium. Cream stock, two ink colours, hairline rules, and a serif that has never apologised for anything. The only light direction in the set, and the only one that treats the game script as writing rather than output.

  • The Program
  • Folio
  • Saturday Edition

Typography

Georgia by three

Playfair Display at 900 for the matchup, Lora for everything read in sentences. Old-style figures in running text, lining figures in the tables — the distinction real print makes and almost no app bothers with.

Playfair Display 900 · Lora 400/600 & italic

Palette

Stock#F2EDE1
Inset#FBF8F1
Ink#1F1A15
Oxblood#8C2A2A
Navy#1B2C4A

Program stock and two inks, oxblood and navy, the way a two-colour press job actually works. No fills, no shadows, no third colour — structure comes entirely from rules and white space.

UI & layout

  • NavRunning heads and a folio at the foot of each screen instead of a tab bar.
  • ResultPaged, not scrolled. Swipe left and right through the sections like turning leaves.
  • ScriptThe three acts are set as real columns with drop caps and a byline for the model.
  • TablesMatrix and distribution set as printed tables — hairline rules, no zebra fills.

Signature move

The printed game script. The three-act narrative the engine already writes gets set as a proper column of type with a drop cap and a dateline — and every number in the prose is a live link back to the table it came from. The analysis reads like a preview article that happens to be generated.

The Book

The app already computes edge against the market on every screen. This direction stops burying it. Felt black, two neons that never touch, and a layout that leads with the disagreement between the model and the line.

  • Moneyline
  • The Book
  • Edge

Typography

MODEL +1.0 vs THE LINE

Chakra Petch for numbers — squared terminals, slight technical edge, reads like a board rather than a brand. Barlow underneath keeps the prose from feeling like machinery.

Chakra Petch 600/700 · Barlow 400/500

Palette

Felt#080A0C
Rail#12161A
Model#22E5D6
Market#FF3D8A
Ink#F2F6F8

Two neons with one job each and no overlap: cyan is always the model, magenta is always the market. Wherever they sit apart on a screen, that gap is the whole product.

UI & layout

  • NavA live odds ticker replaces the header. Tabs sit under it, tight and dense.
  • ResultEdge leads. Win probability is second. Everything else follows in a compact stack.
  • SlateThe whole week sorted by disagreement — biggest gap between model and line at the top.
  • ColourEvery chart draws both series: cyan for the model, magenta ghosted behind it.

Signature move

The disagreement rail. One horizontal axis per game with the market line as a magenta tick and the model as a cyan one. Down the Slate tab, thirty games become thirty rails, and the week’s biggest edges are visible without reading a single number.

07

Field Manual

No cards, no radius, no shadow, no gradient. Newsprint grey, hard black rules and one safety orange. The type is the interface — set enormous, aligned left, and left alone. The riskiest direction here and the one most likely to be remembered.

  • Down & Distance
  • Manual
  • Third & Long

Typography

61.4

Archivo Black at sizes that feel like a mistake until you see them on a phone, tracked to minus four percent. Archivo at 500 and 700 for everything else. One superfamily, two roles, no ornament anywhere.

Archivo Black · Archivo 500/700

Palette

Stock#E8E8E4
Inset#FFFFFF
Rule#0A0A0A
Safety#FF4A00

Four values, and the fourth is doing all the talking. Orange marks exactly one thing per screen — the model’s answer. Every other element is black on grey with a two-pixel rule between it and its neighbour.

UI & layout

  • NavNo tab bar. A sticky index rail down the right edge, numbered, always visible.
  • ResultOne continuous type sheet. Section breaks are two-pixel rules, nothing else.
  • NumbersHeadline figures set at sixty points plus. You read the screen from across the room.
  • ControlsButtons are outlined rectangles with underlined labels. Nothing is rounded.

Signature move

The numbers are the layout. There is no card holding the win probability — the figure itself, set at ninety points in safety orange, is the section. Strip every container and the hierarchy has nowhere to hide, which is exactly the discipline a data product benefits from.

Letterman

Wool navy, felt cream, chenille gold. This is the one direction that is unmistakably college — a hundred and thirty programmes with a hundred and thirty years of insignia between them, and an app that treats a season like something you earn rather than something you query.

  • Varsity
  • Pennant
  • Letterman

Typography

GEORGIA 27

Graduate — an actual collegiate block face — for team names and scores, set in caps with a little air. Oswald handles labels and body, condensed enough to keep long school names on one line.

Graduate 400 · Oswald 400/500/600

Palette

Wool#182339
Panel#202C46
Felt#F5EFE0

Wool navy and felt cream with a gold varsity stripe. Oxblood appears only on the losing side of a number, so the palette itself keeps score.

The pennant wall. The Teams tab abandons the list entirely and becomes a wall of a hundred and thirty pennants in each programme’s real colours. It is the one screen in any of these directions that could only exist for college football, and it makes the CFB app feel native rather than ported.

Node Graph

The engine already has four weighted nodes feeding one margin. This direction stops describing that and starts drawing it — the graph is the home screen, and running a matchup means watching weight flow through it.

  • Havoc
  • Node
  • Fourth Node

Typography

Weighted — 0.35

Syne at 800 for headings, which has enough strangeness in its letterforms to feel engineered rather than corporate. Space Mono carries every figure, so the data always reads as machine output.

Syne 700/800 · Space Mono 400/700

Palette

Void#08060F
Node#120E22
Edge#241C40
Signal#B4FF39
Weight#7B5CFF

A violet void with one electric lime that appears only where the model has produced something itself. Violet carries structure, lime carries conclusions, and nothing on screen is ever both.

UI & layout

  • HomeThe four-node graph, live. Node size is its weight; you drag to retune and the margin moves.
  • ResultTap any node to expand its contribution — the graph is also the navigation.
  • ModelThe Settings tab merges into the graph. Tuning weights stops being a separate screen.
  • MotionRunning a matchup animates weight travelling along the edges into the margin.

Signature move

The graph is the settings screen. Scheme, personnel, environment and X-factor are nodes you physically resize, and the projected margin recomputes under your finger. It collapses two screens into one and makes the model’s structure impossible to misunderstand.

Home Colors

A near-white shell that has no accent of its own and borrows the matchup’s instead. Load Georgia against Alabama and the app turns red and black. Load Michigan against Ohio State and it turns maize and scarlet. The chrome is a neutral stage; the teams are the identity.

  • Sideline
  • Two‑Deep
  • Colors

Typography

Georgia 27.4

Plus Jakarta Sans at 800 and 500, tracked tight on the display sizes. A neutral, extremely legible family is the right call when the colour is going to change under it forty times a Saturday.

Plus Jakarta Sans 500/600/800

Palette

Shell#F6F7F9
Card#FFFFFF
Ink#0F1417
Home*dynamic
Away*dynamic

Three fixed neutrals and two slots. The last two colours are pulled from the two programmes on screen and auto-corrected for contrast, so every matchup looks like its own app without anyone hand-picking a palette.

UI & layout

  • NavStandard five tabs, generous targets, the active tab in the home team’s colour.
  • ResultA card feed. Each section is a rounded card you can reorder and pin to the top.
  • ThemingBoth team colours are lightness-corrected on load so contrast holds against white.
  • ReachEverything reachable one-handed. The Run button lives above the tab bar, always.

Signature move

The app repaints itself per matchup. Colours already live in the team data both apps ship. Read them, run them through a contrast fix, and one build gives you a hundred and thirty visual identities — the cheapest distinctiveness available and the only concept here that scales for free.

Choosing

Where each one earns its keep.

The two apps do not have to land in the same place. The college app has a hundred and thirty programmes and a pageantry problem; the pro app has thirty-two and a credibility problem. A split is a legitimate outcome.

Naming

If the pair gets renamed, rename it as a pair.

Two apps sharing one engine should share one naming logic, so the college app never reads as the afterthought. Four families that hold up:

Saturday & Sunday

Saturday  /  Sunday

The strongest of the four. Two words, no explanation needed, and it says college and pro to anyone who has ever watched a down. Works as a wordmark at any size.

Chalk

Chalk College  /  Chalk Pro

Chalk is the betting term for the favourite and the thing a coordinator writes with. A single syllable carrying both halves of the product is rare.

Snapcount

Snapcount CFB  /  Snapcount NFL

Sounds like the analytics tool it is. Snap counts are a real input the pro engine already uses, so the name is not decorative.

Hashmark

Hashmark  /  Hashmark Pro

Neutral, ownable, and quietly about precise positions on a field. The safest choice if the brand needs to outlive the football framing.
