Round two · Identity, marks & pattern

Marks & Material

Ten identities, each with a drawn logo, an app icon, a repeating pattern, a palette and a type pairing. Where round one proposed screen treatments, this set proposes brands: something you could stamp on a hat, a splash screen and a favicon and have it still be the same thing.

On the last set: half of it was borrowed from software rather than from football — terminal monospace, neon on black, a node graph, the glow. That reads as a dev tool wearing a jersey. Every direction here is drawn from football’s own material culture instead: heraldry, chains, film stock, card stock, leather, brass, ticket paper, survey maps, helmet stripes and wire copy. Real objects with real graphic traditions behind them.

The Crest

Football is the last place in American life that still takes heraldry seriously. Every programme already owns a crest, a motto and a set of colours. This identity behaves like an athletic association rather than a product: inscriptional caps, a real shield, and gold used the way an institution uses gold.

If it were my call

Pigskin, Contour, and The Stripe.

Pigskin has the best mark of the ten and the warmest palette, and the laces solve the icon problem outright. Contour is the only direction whose pattern and whose charts are the same drawing, which means the identity does real work instead of decorating. The Stripe is the one that would still look right in five years, and its per-team helmets give you 130 identities for the price of one. If the two apps split: Drumline or Admit One for college, The Wire or Autumn Reel for pro.

Marks are concepts drawn to communicate a direction, not final artwork — a chosen one should be redrawn on a proper grid with optical corrections and a full size ramp.
